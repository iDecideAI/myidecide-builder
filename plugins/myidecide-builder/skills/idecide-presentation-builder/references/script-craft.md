> narration and on-screen copy. Written as guidance to the writer; apply it to
> your own drafting.

# iDecide Script Craft — corpus-derived writing guide (40 scripts)

Follow these rules exactly. Every rule below was extracted from 40 shipped iDecide scripts (~3,400 rows).
Bracketed numbers cite the source script. Verbatim quotes are house style — reuse and adapt them freely;
they are the brand voice, not plagiarism.

**SCOPE — this document governs VOICE AND CRAFT ONLY: how a beat sounds, how long it runs, how an
interaction is phrased, which house lines to reuse.** It does NOT govern the output's columns,
slide naming, or closing structure — the script contract above does, and it WINS on any conflict.
Where this guide describes the corpus doing something the contract forbids (a hamburger specced as a
production-note row rather than a real terminal slide; menus written as two rows on one slide; answer
forks sharing one Slide#/Part; no Slide Name column), that is an accurate description of the SOURCE
SCRIPTS and an incorrect shape for YOUR OUTPUT. Take the wording from here, the structure from the
contract.

## 1. Voiceover voice & register

- Write as first-person plural "we" (the company) speaking to a singular "you." Never third-person about the viewer.
- Use "I" only when a personal rep narrates: "I'm so excited to get to know you a little" [31].
- Refer to the sender obliquely when relevant: "The person who sent this to you must really care about you" [28].
- Use contractions everywhere ("you'll," "we're," "doesn't"). Drop them only inside legal/compliance sentences.
- Hold each row to ONE beat: median 20–25 words (~8 seconds of VO), HARD
  CEILING ~30 words (≈10 seconds). CRITICAL: in the builder, ALL Parts
  sharing one Slide Name merge into ONE narration on ONE set of graphics —
  so a standard content slide gets ONE Part (~≤30 words TOTAL). Anything
  longer becomes a NEW SLIDE NAME ("<Topic> - 2", "Welcome - 2"), never
  just a new Part. MORE, SHORTER slides always beats one long sit. A
  4-sentence welcome (thanks + "this is interactive" + big-picture line +
  lead-in) is TWO slides. Only two exceptions may run long: a quote being
  read in full, and a beat where a provided video plays through.
  (Interactive slides — menus, questions, CTAs — hold ≥15s by design;
  still one row each, but their VO may run a little fuller.)
- Rows over 40 words are reserved for two cases only: menu invites and dense legal/finance explanations.
- Mark vocal stress with ALL-CAPS single words inside a lowercase sentence:
  - "you'll see only what YOU'RE interested in seeing" [03][28]
  - "The only thing missing is YOU!" [07][25][30]
- Almost no passive voice anywhere. Short sentences; fragments are allowed for rhythm:
  - "Instant impact. A cup of happy. A dose of healthy." [01][02]
  - "Not tomorrow. Not 'someday'. But starting today!" [02]

Each beat type has its own register:

- **Hooks** — warm, short, promise-forward, exclamation-marked:
  - "Hello and welcome to this short, interactive experience!" [09]
  - "What if you could live a happier life?" [02]
  - "You're about to see something most business owners never hear about once and immediately wish they'd known years ago." [37]
  - The signature intro claim, reused across a third of the corpus: "we believe you may one day look back at
    this very moment as a turning point in your life" [05][08][16][18][19][21][24][32] — always paired with the
    name/location/date personalization tokens.
  - The interactivity promise belongs in the hook: "This short presentation is interactive, so you'll be asked to
    tap your screen from time to time, to see only what YOU'RE interested in seeing. If you're on a computer
    you'll need to click." [03]
- **Explanations** — flat declarative, no exclamations, one fact per row, often ending on a clause that sets up the next row.
- **Stat beats** — number, then a translation, then punch. Never state a big number without dramatizing it:
  - "78%! That means almost 8 out of 10 can't afford to miss even ONE paycheck!" [16][35]
  - "Would you believe it was 180 billion dollars? And that's expected to increase to $300 Billion by 2025!" [07]
  - "they would stretch over 74,000 miles — enough to circle the Earth nearly two and a half times!" [17]
- **Transitions** — 4–10 words, a gear-shift interjection opening the row:
  - "So let's jump right in…" [10]; "Well let's break it down." [11]
  - "Ok enough of the depressing stuff…" [16]; "But enough with the depressing stats. Let's get to the good stuff… the solutions!" [39]
  - "But forget about them. What about YOU?" [40]; "That's where we come in!" [24]
- **Question setups** — soften, then give the physical instruction, then "please":
  - "Go ahead and slide the button to your answer please." [16][20]
  - "Take a guess as to what percentage of bankruptcies are due to illness." [19]
- **Answer responses** — one beat of validation, then immediate forward motion (see §3).
- **Closers** — every Finish Up opens with the fixed recap line: "So by now you're probably getting a better
  understanding of why [people everywhere are talking about X / X is growing so fast]" [02][03][04][05][06][08][09][31].
  Recap 2–4 proof beats, then the closing fork. Binary-future closes work well before the fork:
  - "So what will you be saying in 5 years? 'I'm glad I did' or 'I wish I would've'?" [25][26]
  - "So five years from now, will these still be goals? Or will they be realities?" [27]
- End terminal slides with "Have a great day!" — the near-universal sign-off — or "welcome to [Company]!"
- Delivery directions go in parentheses inside the Script cell: "(jokingly in infomercial voice)" [04];
  "(rhetorical)" [19]; "(JOKINGLY)" [17].
- Humor is one-liners bolted to content, never standalone: "you can't sell it if you can smell it!" [12];
  "It's almost like having Fun Insurance!" [04]; "But wait… there's more! (jokingly in infomercial voice)" [04].

## 2. Engagement interaction vocabulary (the full menu — use several per deck)

For EVERY interaction: the VO names the physical action ("tap," "click," "slide," "drag," "rub," "swipe," "enter"),
the On Screen column carries the bare option list plus routing, and EVERY branch gets its own VO row prefixed
with its condition in caps. Branch rows share the same Slide#/Part# and carry distinct audio labels (§10).

- **Single-select multiple choice (2–6 options).** OS lists bare labels with destinations:
  - `YES - SLIDE 2 / NO - SLIDE 11` [10]; `OF COURSE! (part 10) / SKEPTICAL, BUT YES. (next Part)` [04]
  - Relative jumps allowed: `YES - next part / NO - part 14` [17].
  - Add floating wrong-answer feedback where playful: `FLOATING BUBBLES THAT SHOW "ALMOST!", "TRY AGAIN"` [16][35];
    per-option nudges `20% try again / 50% almost / 80% (correct answer) / 100% not quite, some of it is genetic` [31].
  - Bias options toward yes when consensus-building: `OF COURSE! / NO, I ENJOY OVERPAYING!` [06]; `DUH! OF COURSE! / I'M NOT SURE` [07].
  - Forced-answer gag for beliefs the script needs: `(ARROW POINTING TO HIGHER WHEN THEY TRY TO CHOOSE LOWER OR SAME)` [40].
- **Multi-select.** "Go ahead and choose as many as you'd like!" [01][24][27]; "Click all that apply, please." [26];
  "Tap all that apply." [29]. End the list with `All Of These!`. Always script the under-select scold branch:
  - `IF THEY CHOOSE ONLY 1- "That's it?! Dream a little and choose at least 2!"` [18][19][20]
  - "(slide2_p7_Dream) That's it? It's OK to dream a little!" [02]
  - Record two payoff variants: "Well you'll be happy to know that we have those!" (partial) vs
    "Fantastic! You'll be happy to know that we have ALL of them!" (all) [19].
  - Exclusive options are annotated: `No clue. (unselect all)` [03]; "(this will unselect everything)" [29].
- **Sliders.** VO says "slide the button"; OS shows dash-drawn endpoints:
  - `20%------60%` [35]; `$1 Billion - $180 Billion` [07]; `14%------78%` [16]
  - Labeled endpoints: `1-------10 / Not at All  Totally Satisfied` [18]; optionally list discrete stops beneath: `$500----$10,000 / 500 / 2500 / 3750…` [20].
- **Drag-and-drop ranking.** "…drag the one that's most important to YOU to the top please." [25];
  magic-wand framing: "if we could wave a magic wand and improve just one thing, what would it be?" [18][31].
  Always call the answer back later (§6): `(SHOW ANSWER DRAGGED TO TOP)` [25].
- **Swipe.** "Swipe right if you agree or left if you don't." [18]; "Swipe left if wrong, and right if it's correct." [35].
- **Rub-to-reveal.** The Logo Reveal staple. Always include the desktop fallback sentence:
  - "Go ahead and rub your screen to see the company that's doing just that… all over the world!" [02]
  - "Left click and drag your mouse if you're on a computer, please." [05][22][31]
  - OS: `LOGO REVEAL` or `RUB YOUR SCREEN`. The reveal line always teases the mission, never names the company —
    the graphic delivers the name; the NEXT row speaks it: "ASEA. Bringing the incredible Redox Signaling technology to the world…" [05].
  - Invent themed variants: "smash the old you" shatter [01]; "tap your screen to crack the glass and see the
    answer" / `REVEAL "MONEY!"` [04]; glass-break `'30 YEARS' TO REVEAL '7 YEARS!'` [39].
- **Hover / click-to-expand panels.** The dominant content-delivery move. One VO row invites
  ("Click to learn about each please." [01][14]), then one row PER panel tagged `(HOVER NO VO)` or `HOVER WITH VO`,
  panel copy in Script or OS. Panels carry a title + 1–3 sentence body. Keep buttons on screen during panel
  videos "so they can choose something else midway if they'd like" [31].
- **Calculators / number entry.** VO invites playfully ("Let's have some fun with this interactive calculator…" [33]),
  hedges ("Naturally, these are just based on company averages and results will vary" [33]), and OS carries blanks
  and the formula VERBATIM for the builder:
  - `CALCULATOR (200 gallons x their price per gallon x .20 = savings)` [06]
  - `MONTHLY INCOME- (= number entered x $1680) / YEARLY INCOME - (=monthly income x 12)` [19]
  - `(= (AxB) x 30. Ex: (10,000 reps x 20%) x 30 = $60,000)` [07]
  - `TOTAL SHORTFALL = (Debt + Income + Mortgage + Education) – Current` [27]
  - Slider inputs get increments: `Income: $50,000 ---- $1 million (in $50k increments if possible)` [27]
  - Then a payoff row that reads the result: "Based on your answer, Green Fuel Tabs could save you ________!" [06].
  - Gate optional calculators: "Do you want to take a guess at what that total would be using our simple calculator?"
    `IF YES, SHOW CALCULATOR. IF NO- SKIP TO "THIS ALLOWS…"` [13].
- **IF/THEN branches.** Prefix the Script cell in caps:
  - `IF CORRECT-` / `IF INCORRECT-` [06][08]; `IF YES-` / `IF NO-` [10]
  - Value-specific: `IF $50:` / `IF ANYTHING OTHER THAN $50:` [04]; `IF 50%-` / `IF ANYTHING BUT 50%-` [18]
  - Attitude branches: `(IF SKEPTICAL, BUT YES: Thank you for your honesty! This is important stuff that could literally change your life! Let's do it!)` [04]
  - Graduated bands for near-misses: `IF 76-100 or 101-126- Not bad! / IF 51-75- Good guess! / IF 5-50- Sorry. Not even close.` [03]
  - Compound conditions written longhand: `IF ANYTHING OTHER THAN "NO ONE" IN Q2, 2 OR HIGHER IN Q4 Q5 Q6, AND YES IN Q3:` [28]
  - Mark reconvergence explicitly: `IF NO or AFTER ABOVE` [07]; `(IF NO OR AFTER YES)` [17].
- **Yes/No detour gates.** Offer optional depth mid-section:
  - "Would you like to spend a minute learning why…?" `YES - slide 9 / NO - slide 10` [32]
  - "Would you like to see some more featured styles?" `YES (NEXT PART) / NO (PART 14)` [07]
  - Mid-video attention check before long embeds: "Would you like to learn more?" `YES (next part) / NO (part 4)` [25].
- **Qualification funnels.** Sequential Yes/No gates, each with a kind disqualify slide and a rep notification:
  `YES - Next part / NO - disqualified no taxes (s13) / text notif 2 - did not pay taxes last year` [37]. Blame the
  rule, not the person: "you probably wouldn't qualify because it's a tax credit, so you have to have paid taxes
  in order to get a credit." [38]. Always include `Continue Anyway` [38].
- **Personalization tokens.** OS-only, caps: `SHOW VIEWER'S NAME`, `SHOW VIEWER'S LOCATION`, `SHOW DATE/TIME`,
  `(SHOW THEIR LOCATION USING GEOLOCATION FEATURE)` [01]. Write the VO so it works with or without the token:
  - "People in big cities, small towns… even in your very own neighborhood!" [01]
  - "And no matter where you are in the world" [06][08]; "Probably hundreds in your town alone!" [39]
  - "Not tomorrow. Not 'someday'. But starting today!" + `Insert Time/Date Stamp` [02]
  - Name drops inside OS copy: `because of you, name!` [19]; `Thanks, name!` [06].
- **Media.** Embed videos with timecodes in OS: `(1:40-3:45) https://youtu.be/…` [05]; `AUTO START VIDEO WITH SKIP
  OPTION` [05]; force-play then advance: "(Force video to play then go to next part)" [08]; iframe live shop pages
  with cropping notes [07]; scrollable PDFs with "You can also click the DOWNLOAD button to download it for future
  reference." [05]; copy-to-clipboard utility: "You can also click the COPY button to save it to your clipboard and
  use it as a checklist later if you like!" [11].

## 3. Question craft

- Front-load 3–5 engagement questions in the Intro; frame them as a favor to the viewer, never data collection:
  - "So we can custom-tailor this presentation specifically to you…" [17]
  - "Each answer will affect the next question and the ultimate outcome, so everything is totally customized just for you!" [01]
  - "First, so we honor your time, let's see if you even NEED an estate plan…" [28]
- Typical counts by genre: consumer product decks 3–5 intro questions + 1–2 per section; recruiting/opportunity
  decks front-load harder (up to 15 profiling questions [20]); education decks may use zero profiling and instead
  embed rhetorical teaching questions throughout [21]. Progress-meter long stacks: `SHOW "1 of 4"` … `"4 of 4"` [28].
- Prefer "do you think / guess how much" phrasing over quiz phrasing:
  - "Guess how many different diets the average adult will try in their lifetime?" [03]
  - "what percent of Americans do you think are living paycheck to paycheck?" [16]
- Gameshow framing keeps stacks fun: "let's have some fun and do a 'speed round' of 5 questions." [21];
  "Let's warm things up with a few quick true-or-false questions" [22].
- Signpost the runway: number them ("1.", "2." [01]) or count down: "Just 2 more." [05]; "Okay, last question!" [07];
  "Thank you. Last one." [27].
- **Correct answers:** celebrate in 2–4 words, then immediately restate the fact so both branches deliver it:
  - "That's right! Leading analysts predict that gas prices could soar to $10/gallon! Let's do another." [06]
  - "You nailed it! By age 30 only HALF our stem cells are still active." [18]
  - When being right is bad news: "Unfortunately, you're right." [16][39].
- **Incorrect answers:** NEVER say "wrong." Soften with:
  - "Close, but…" [06]; "Actually," [18][35]; "Would you believe…" [06][39]
  - "That would have been awesome, but…" [18]
  - "A lot of people think that! And it would be worth every penny at that cost! But…" [04]
  - The fact line after the branch word is IDENTICAL to the correct branch — only the opener differs.
- **Opinion answers** get thanks, not grades: "Thanks for your honesty!" [18][20]; "Great—thank you for sharing that." [17].
  Self-rating sliders get a matched low/high pair: "Ok thanks! Let's see if we can get that to a 10!" vs
  "Wow! That's fantastic! But there must be SOMETHING you're not 100% happy with." [18][31].
- **Non-fit answers** get grace + referral + escape hatch: "Thanks for your honesty… it probably wouldn't be a fit" [20]
  with a `CONTINUE ANYWAY` button (§7).
- Yes-momentum stacks work: 4–5 consecutive "do you think most people would like to improve…?" `OF COURSE / NO`
  rows before the reveal [05], capped with the pivot question "if YOU could be the one to help people improve their
  lives in any or ALL of those areas, would that excite you?" [05].
- Answer-tiered welcome: one question, three response recordings — "Got it! Looks like you're in the right place." (1–5) /
  "Awesome! Sounds like you're on the right track." (6–8) / "You rock! You're on top of your health goals" (9–10) [01].

## 4. Menu craft

- Write TWO menu VO lines, recorded as separate audio files. Put the label in the Script cell:
  `(slide3_p1_first)`, `_first.mp3`.
- **First visit** — the fixed formula is [you're in control] + [you can come back to the others later] +
  [which would you like to see first?]:
  - "Go ahead and choose what you'd like to learn about, please. You can come back and look at the others later
    if you like, but which would you like to see first?" [02][05][06][33][37]
  - "Simply tap on what you'd like to see first and remember you can always come back to the main menu to view a
    different selection." [03]
  - Recommendation variant after profiling: "Based on your answers, THESE are the topics we think you'd be most
    interested in, but feel free to choose whatever you'd like!" [18][24]
  - Guided variant: "Not sure where to start? Try 'Business Model'—most agents begin there." [25]
- **Repeat visit** — shorter, always names the exit:
  - "Go ahead and choose another topic to learn about, or click Finish Up when you're ready to move on." [04][22][36]
  - Sub-menu version: "Go ahead and choose another product, or click move ahead when you're ready!" [02]
- Option labels: 1–3 words, Title Case, noun phrases — they become BUTTONS, and
  a label is capped at 22 characters and never truncated ("Am I Eligible?" [37];
  "Is Cloud Computing Right For My Business?" [09]; "Move Yourself or Hire Movers?" [13]). Last item is ALWAYS the
  exit: `Finish Up` on the Main Menu, `Move Ahead` on sub-menus.
- Notate destinations inline in OS after each label — any of these styles works, but be consistent within a deck:
  `(SLIDE 4)`, `- SLIDE 4`, `s4`, `(s4)`, bare `- 3`. Conditional destinations are allowed:
  `- COMPENSATION (IF CANADA SLIDE 14) (IF US SLIDE 20)` [25].
- The first-visit menu is the LAST part of the Intro slide; the Main Menu revisit lives on its OWN slide whose
  only row is the repeat line + the same OS list. Repeat the full menu list in OS at every gate.
- Personalize menus with highlight rules in OS: `(HIGHLIGHT BASED ON ANSWERS ABOVE)`;
  `Tax Free Retirement (ALWAYS HIGHLIGHT)` [20]; `(HIGHLIGHT IF THEY SAID YES TO LAST QUESTION)` [18];
  formula blocks: `*RECOMMENDATION FORMULA. -ALWAYS HIGHLIGHT "PRODUCTS" AND "TESTIMONIALS" -IF THEY ANSWER YES
  TO INCOME- ALSO HIGHLIGHT "EARNING POTENTIAL"` [24].
- Sub-menus (products, compensation, concepts) nest ONE level; their exit is `Move Ahead` returning to the Main
  Menu slide: `- Move Ahead (SLIDE 5)` [05]. Sub-menus get their own first/repeat pair.

## 4b. Sliders & multi-select — one follow-up, never branches

Single-answer button questions branch (Correct / Incorrect / per-choice).
Sliders and multi-select questions (pick several, then Move Ahead) advance to
ONE slide only — write a single follow-up beat that reveals the correct
answer or responds generally, never per-answer beats.

## 5. Section-gate lines (they live on MENU slides, not chapter endings)

**BINDING (this pipeline):** the gate sentence is spoken by the **Main Menu -
Return slide itself** (or a sub-menu's own slide) — NEVER by a chapter's final
beat. A chapter's last slide reads like any other content line — its VO and
visuals mirror only that slide's content, with NO navigation language ("move
ahead", "click", "when you're ready", "choose another topic") and no
buttons — then auto-advances to Main Menu - Return, which delivers the ONE
gate line. Two back-to-back gate VOs is a defect. The ONLY exception: a
user-specified interactive close (e.g. "click each testimonial") where the
static/hover-content gate phrasing below belongs on that interactive slide.

Reuse ONE gate sentence per deck rather than writing bespoke ones. Canonical variants:
- "Go ahead and choose another topic, or Finish Up when you're ready to move on." [04][06][38] (+ OS repeats the full MAIN MENU list)
- "Go ahead and choose something else to learn about, or FINISH UP to move on." [07]
- "Go ahead and choose another topic to learn about, or click Finish Up to wrap up." [02][05]
- Static-content gate: "Take a look and click Move Ahead when you're ready!" [14][25][32]
- Hover-content gate: "Click each area to learn more then click move ahead when you're ready to move on." [33]
- Charming one-off gates are allowed if reused deck-wide: "Did something catch your eye?" [31].
- Silent gates are OS-only: `BACK TO MAIN MENU`, `Back to s4`, `AUTO Play video - go back to main menu when done` [18][25][28].
- Mid-section soft-close gates may offer conversion early: "Would you like to take 2 minutes to start the process?"
  `YES - GO TO START YOUR CLAIM NOW SECTION / NOT YET - BACK TO MAIN MENU` [10] — label the decline "NOT YET," never "NO."
- Cross-sell gates point to sibling sections: "Please be sure to watch the GET PAID HELPING PEOPLE section to learn
  how YOU can be the one to help change their lives." [39].

## 6. Callbacks & continuity (plant early, pay off in Finish Up)

- Capture in the intro, replay at the close. OS notation is an ALL-CAPS imperative naming the source:
  - `SHOW THE PICS THEY CHOSE EARLIER` [04]; `SHOW THEIR CHOICES HERE` [01]
  - `SHOW THEIR INCOME GOAL FROM THE QUESTION ABOVE` [07]; `SHOW #1 CONCERN FROM DRAG AND DROP QUESTION` [18]
  - `(SHOW THEIR ANSWER TO QUESTION #2 ABOVE)` [31]; with slide/part pointer: `[DYNAMIC RESPONSES] / S2P1` [27]
  - Named variables for heavy reuse: `[SEASON] [COMPANION] [VACATION]` set at capture and replayed:
    "You love to travel in the [SEASON], [COMPANION] and you're ready for a [VACATION]." [26].
- The strongest close: show their subset, then reveal the full set —
  "Remember earlier you said THESE were important to you?" `show what they chose` → "We challenge you to…
  build a business with ALL of these things." `make all of them show` [19][25];
  "Well you'll be happy to know that Savvi provides ALL of these and more!" [07].
- Write deictic VO so the graphic carries the noun: "we can help YOU accomplish THIS" + `SHOW ANSWER DRAGGED TO TOP` [18][25].
- Verbal callbacks need no data: "Remember we asked if someone has ever told you they love your outfit…?
  Well that stops today" [07]; "Remember the Rule of 72 we talked about earlier?" [21]; "Remember that breakthrough
  discovery we spoke about earlier?" [05]. Plant forward teases too: "More on that later!" [40].
- Persist answers for the rep: `STORE ANSWER IN NOTIFICATIONS!!!` [32]; `(SHOW THEIR ANSWERS IN REP NOTIFICATIONS)` [19];
  completion pings per close path: `Pres Complete Notif 1` [29].
- Route content off answers with mapping tables in OS: `If A → SHOW RESET & AWAKEN / If B → SHOW DETOX & AWAKEN…` [24];
  lane pattern: "Based on your answers, it looks like THIS is what you'd be most interested in…" with the payload
  swapped per lane (`OUR INCREDIBLE SKINCARE PRODUCTS` / `MAKEUP` / `STARTING A BUSINESS`) [31].
- Confirm captured data before using it in math: "Earlier you indicated that you take this many vacations per
  year… Is this correct?" [26].

## 7. Terminal slide craft

- **Get Started walkthrough** — the fixed 3–4 beat formula, ONE beat per Part (each Part matches a screenshot):
  1. "Fantastic! In a moment you'll click the Get Started button on your screen."
  2. "That will take you to a page that looks like this. Just enter your info and you're all set!"
  3. optional: "Simply choose the option that's best for you and follow the simple steps." / "Select retail or
     subscribe and save – remember you can modify or cancel… at any time." [03]
  4. "Go ahead and click Get Started, and welcome to [Company]!" [01][02][08][18][31][32]
  - Long enrollments get survival tips: "Be sure you have at least 5 minutes to finish… BEFORE YOU START, WRITE
    DOWN YOUR PASSWORD NOW AND KEEP IT HANDY." [03]
- **Schedule A Call** — setup line is house-verbatim: "Great! So we can avoid the endless back and forth of missed
  calls and voicemails, please enter your info." [01][03][08][09][31][35]. Parts 2–3 are `No audio` form/scheduler
  screens (`STEP 2 OF 3 (NO VOICE OVER)` [39]). Confirmation: "Fantastic! We'll call you then, but feel free to
  reach out to us as well! Have a great day!" [01][08]. Write paired rows `WITH CALENDLY` (click-the-button
  version, filename `_click.mp3`) and `WITHOUT CALENDLY` (enter-your-info version) [24][26][28][32].
  OS: `APPOINTMENT SETTING GRAPHIC` + `SHOW REP'S INFO ON SCREEN`.
- **Not Right Now / decline** — always three moves: thanks, door open, referral ask:
  - "No problem! We're here for you when you're ready! Have a great day!" [04]
  - "please pass this along to other business owners you know. We're sure they'd appreciate it…" [10]
  - Deadline sting where honest: "we can only go back and claim your credits for the last 3 years, so waiting too
    long may result in missing out" [37]. Never guilt.
  - Add a `CONTINUE ANYWAY` or `ANSWER QUESTION AGAIN` button in OS that re-enters the flow [03][05][39][40].
- **The closing fork** (last Finish Up row) lists 3–5 action-named options, each its own terminal slide:
  `-TRY THE PRODUCTS (SLIDE 13) -EARN MONEY REFERRING OTHERS (SLIDE 14) -JOIN AS A BRAND PARTNER TODAY (SLIDE 15)
  -SCHEDULE A CALL FOR MORE INFO (SLIDE 16)` [02]. Ask it as: "So go ahead and click your screen to get started,
  or schedule a call to have your questions answered!" [08]. A second-chance ladder may follow a decline:
  decline → "Not a problem! Are you ready to order…?" → still-no → options menu ending `No Thank You` [17].
- **URLs & rep info** live in OS, never spoken. Note the replicated-link mechanics for the builder:
  - `(https://example.com/acmehealth/products) acmehealth is the username` [01]
  - `("jordan" is the rep identifier)` [03]; `D1 IS THE REP'S "SPONSOR ID"` [08]
  - `GOES TO REP'S CALENDLY` [20][27]; `Book a Zoom (goes to rep's calendly)` [16]
  - `SHOW REALTOR'S CONTACT NAME, EMAIL, PHONE, SOCIAL MEDIA HANDLES AND PICTURE` [12]

## 8. On-screen text discipline

- On Screen is the KEY PHRASE, never the transcript. A 30-word VO row gets 2–7 on-screen words:
  - VO "The result was a $5.54 billion settlement…" → OS `$5.54 billion settlement` [10]
  - VO "Right now, 70% of the world is battling preventable health issues…" → OS `70%` [33]
  - A 62-word compliance paragraph reduces to seven fragments: `WFG / financial services company / step-by-step
    process / …` [21]
- Leave OS EMPTY when nothing new appears — an empty cell means "hold the current visual." Most explanation rows are empty.
- Transcribe 1:1 only when the VO literally reads a bullet list — then paste the exact bullets [04][32].
- Use ALL-CAPS imperative verbs for build instructions: `SHOW`, `REVEAL`, `HIGHLIGHT`, `ADD`, `REMOVE`, `REUSE`:
  - `(SHOW $4,590 ON SCREEN)` [17]; `SHOW PREVIOUS GRAPHIC BUT BRING EACH FORWARD AS VO SAYS THEM` [35]
  - Animation direction is verbs, not adjectives: `gas sign ticks to $10` [06]; `counter up to 55` [21];
    `thermometer that fills up and counts from 600-1200 color goes from orange to red` [06].
- Button-count shorthand heads option lists: `5 BTN` [03]; `7 BTN MAIN MENU` [03].
  Cap a menu at 8 — the layout holds no more, and every row is required to render.
- Numbered reveals sequence silent hover items: `(0)`–`(7)` on the Finish Up product wall [01];
  `HOVER SECTION 0`–`9` [03]; `1/7`…`7/7` paged documents [13]; `SHOW QUOTES ONE AT A TIME ON SCREEN (1/4, 2/4…)` [37].
- Reference assets, don't describe them twice: `SEE ATTACHMENT` (+ file in Attachments column) [08][12][14];
  reuse pointers `re use from s9p3` [17]; `show videos from s2p3` [04]; `USE THE SLIDES FROM THE PPT` [17].
- Disclaimers live ONLY in OS, never in VO: `(PLACE THIS DISCLAIMER AT THE BOTTOM OF ANSWER SLIDES)` + source URLs [16][35];
  income disclaimers pasted beside every earnings claim [03][24][32]; product disclaimers under health claims [18].
- Row-status markers you may encounter and must respect when revising: `(NEW)`, `EDIT`, `DELETE`, `UNUSED`
  (row retained, copy dead), `[REUSE]`, `(RENAMED)`, `was s10` renumbering notes [06][09][20][33].
  Never build from a DELETE/UNUSED row; keep part numbers stable when retiring rows (`DELETE / BLANK` [20]).

## 8b. Shortcodes never enter the Script column (binding)

[viewer-name-first], [sender-name], [sender-name-first], [sender-name-last], [sender-email],
[sender-scheduling-url], [current-time], [current-date] resolve in on-screen
TEXT only. The sender's real name and address are never asked for and never
written out — the account supplies them at play time. In a VO row
they are read aloud or dropped — either way the line breaks. Keep spoken
greetings generic ("Hi there", "Great to have you here") and let On Screen
carry the personalized token.

## 9. Pacing

- ~20–25 words per VO row (~8 seconds), hard ceiling ~30 (≈10s) — split
  longer beats into a NEW SLIDE NAME (parts merge into one narration per
  slide in the builder). Fast-cut decks run 15–17 [32]; explanation-heavy
  verticals up to 28 [28]. More, shorter slides > one long slide; only a
  full quote read or a play-through video earns a long sit, and
  interactive slides hold ≥15s by design.
- BUILDER DECKS: every slide = ONE row (multi-part is the logo reveal
  only — see the format contract); grow topics as "<Topic> - 1/2/3…"
  instead of stacking Parts. Interactive slides (menus, questions, CTAs)
  keep one row too — their VO may run a little fuller since they hold
  ≥15s, but the row stays one beat. (The 12–23-Part intro pattern in the
  legacy corpus examples predates this builder — do not reproduce it.)
- One section = one slide (plus its Parts). Decks run 8–26 slides total: single-topic consumer decks 8–15;
  multi-lane product/opportunity decks 14–26. Content sections run 5–19 rows; hooks 3–6 rows before questions;
  Finish Up 4–13 rows; terminal slides 2–5 rows.
- Long hover-panel rows (up to 160 words) are fine — they're read only if clicked, not linear narration.
- Write `(NO VOICE OVER)` rows in exactly these five places:
  1. Slide 1 Part 1 `(NO VOICE OVER) Get Started` — universal cover row.
  2. Hover/expander panels where on-screen text does the talking (`(HOVER NO VO) MEDICAL DEBT` [40]).
  3. Paged reference documents and testimonial walls (`HOVER SECTION 1 OMG Mary Omg! I just hit 9 pounds…` [03]).
  4. `No audio` form/scheduler steps on terminal slides (`scheduler 2 OF 3` [40]).
  5. Auto-playing testimonial/embed videos (`NO VOICEOVER / Patrick- Fast Food Industry` [32]).
  Everywhere else, every row speaks.
- Sequence per deck: hook (3–6 short rows) → intro questions (3–15 rows) → logo reveal (1 row) → credibility
  beats (2–8 rows) → first-visit menu (1 row) → sections each ending in a gate → Finish Up recap → closing fork →
  terminal slides. Deliberate silence is a tool: `STOP MUSIC AFTER 'SECOND' AND START AGAIN AT NEXT LINE FOR EFFECT` [37].

## 10. Production conventions every script must carry

- Open every script with the two boilerplate header rows (no Slide #):
  - `PRODUCTION NOTE: Please add the date that each script version is FIRST published live to the tab title…`
    OS: `EX: Script V1 (4/26/23) / Script V2 (6/03/23)`.
  - `VOICE OVER TALENT NOTE: Each line is split up in a certain way to aid us in the very specific animation
    process and should be recorded in a way that allows for easy separation, but natural flow. Please label each
    file according to the Slide# and Part# making sure all letters are lower case and in this form "slide2_p1.mp3"
    or "slide13_p14.mp3"`.
  - Optionally a third branding/casting row: VO gender/tone ("inviting, warm voice that builds trust, but as least
    salesy as possible… Posture and confidence is more important than excitement." [32]), brand-site references,
    audience-culture notes [39], pronunciation keys ("muni (PRONOUNCED MEW-KNEE)" [35]; "Try- BU -tin" [38]).
- When one Slide#/Part# needs multiple recordings (branches, first/repeat, click variants, tiered responses),
  give each row the SAME Slide/Part and put the audio label in the Script cell:
  - `(slide2_p3_absolutely)` / `(slide2_p3_no)` [02]; `(Label: slide2_p2_correct)` / `_incorrect` [07]
  - `slide3_p1_first` [35]; `(slide10_p1_click)` [09]; `(rename audio "slide13_got_it")` [01]
  - Condition-suffixed: `(NAME: slide2_p3_RLT)` / `(NAME: slide2_p3_SNS)` [28]; `slide2_p9_LESS.mp3` / `slide2_p9_ALL.mp3` [19].
- Mark speculative lines: `ALTERNATE RECORDING VO:` + OS `DON'T BUILD GRAPHICS FOR THIS LINE BUT INCLUDE…
  SO SHE CAN RECORD VO FOR POSSIBLE LATER USE` [03].
- Testimonials: introduce with "It's one thing when you hear it from us, but totally different when you hear real
  stories from real people" framing [24][26], then `(NO VOICEOVER)` rows, one per testimonial, formatted
  quote + em-dash + first name/initial: `"…saved almost $4,000 on a condo in Maui…" - Jon` [04]; `— Dave S.` [32].
  Give each a 2–4 word identity-hook title: `Fat Funeral Miranda`, `Chicken Sue` [03]; `Erin- Stay At Home Mom` [32].
  Video testimonials = Name + URL + timecodes in OS [05][17]. Always attach a results-vary disclaimer.
- Recurring persuasion moves worth reusing:
  - Risk-reversal: "60 day, no questions asked money back guarantee!" [08]; "nothing to lose and total peace of mind to gain." [35]
  - Price-anchor sequence: invite a high guess → stack value → slash to real price → per-day cost:
    "at less than a cup of coffee a day, can you afford NOT to have a BEMER in your home?" [17]; "it costs about
    the same as a latte from your favorite coffee shop!" [08].
  - Three-options close: do nothing / do it yourself / let us help — with the DIY option framed by analogy
    ("just like you have the choice of filing your taxes yourself or representing yourself in a court case") [10].
  - The control promise in the intro: "you're in control every step of the way by clicking the menu icon here" [09][18][28].
  - Pro Tip / Rule of Thumb inserts for education decks: "Here's a helpful rule of thumb: In every room, stand in
    the doorway and look at it through the eyes of a buyer." [12].
- The Hamburger dropdown is specced as a production-note row listing entries + destinations, ending with
  `Restart Presentation (restart)` [31]; add high-value shortcuts ("Include 'Apply For Free' in hamburger menu" [37]).

## Quick self-check before delivering a script

1. Every interactive row: VO names the physical action + OS lists options with destinations + every branch has its
   own conditioned VO row (same Slide/Part, distinct audio label) that reconverges explicitly.
2. Every stat is dramatized; every wrong answer is corrected without "wrong"; every opinion is thanked.
3. Menus: `_first` + repeat lines both written; Finish Up is the last option; gates reuse one sentence; OS repeats
   the full menu list at every gate.
4. At least one callback planted in the intro pays off in Finish Up ("you said THESE… we have ALL of these").
5. Terminal slides follow the walkthrough / Schedule-A-Call / decline formulas verbatim-adjacent, each ending
   "Have a great day!" or "welcome to X!", with a CONTINUE ANYWAY path on every decline.
6. OS column: key phrases only, caps for build commands, disclaimers placed, URLs carry rep-identifier notes,
   empty cells wherever the visual holds.

## Repetition is the tell of a generated script (Bren's tester, 2026-08-24)

A viewer forgives a plain sentence; they notice the third "quick one". Rules:
- No stock phrase twice in one deck. If you catch yourself reaching for the
  same opener, connector, or sign-off, the second use must be rewritten.
- Ban list for filler crutches: "quick one", "let's dive in", "simply put",
  "here's the thing", "at the end of the day", "the best part?" — one use
  each at most, zero is better.
- Vary rhythm: if three consecutive slides open with a question, the fourth
  cannot. If two open with the brand name, break the run.
- Read the finished script START TO END as one performance before emitting.
  Repetition that survives that read is the thing the client's tester will
  quote back at them.
